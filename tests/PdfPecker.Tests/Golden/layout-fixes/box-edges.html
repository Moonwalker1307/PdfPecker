Boxes at page ends

Each section puts a box near the end of a page. A page never ends between a box's top border and its first line, nor between its last line and its bottom border: the box moves to the next page, or takes its last lines along.

Planning starts with the content: which documents are generated, how often, and by whom. Templates are written in HTML and CSS, so the people who design them need no other tool, and the same template serves every customer.

Page breaks follow the rules a browser uses when it prints: headings stay with the paragraph after them, and lines are not left alone at the top or the bottom of a page.

Tables repeat their header rows on every page, and rows are not split unless they are taller than a page. Images keep their aspect ratio and move to the next page whole when they do not fit at the end of one.

Fonts are embedded in the document, subset to the characters it uses, so that it looks the same everywhere and stays small. Right-to-left scripts are shaped and ordered as a browser would show them.

Each release is compared with the one before on a set of documents, and a difference in output is either an intended change or a bug. The documents in this test are some of them.
